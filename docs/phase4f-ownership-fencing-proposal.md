# Phase 4F-B: ownership and fencing contract proposal

Baseline: `3bfd2abea430bc8036105ce85adfdc78706d367d`. Branch: `feat/phase4f-kv-recovery`. The original Phase 4E and 4F failure artifacts and tests remain intact. This review precedes any lease, owner, cursor, ACK, target, or native Trust behavior change. Distributed enforcement remains disabled.

## Observed cause and event boundaries

The retained three fresh-state SIGKILL plus broker-restart trials each had one enrolled dry-run agent and two scoped withdrawals behind ordinary findings. The worker connected within about 0.4 s of restart, saw 30 rejected KV `create()` attempts, and claimed the expired 30 s FILE KV lease at about 30.5 s. Its first critical receipt followed about 50 ms later, then SQLite denial in about 3 ms. An agent-alive broker-restart control denied in 1.76 s. The independent validator repeated the fault at 30.589 s. This establishes the retained broker lease as the dominant measured delay in this one-host fault, without establishing a safe transfer mechanism.

Separate events are: (1) HTTP withdrawal submission; (2) coordinator PostgreSQL journal/outbox commit, which is durable Mesh intake; (3) independent T4/T5 witnessed native intake, not exercised by this dry-run harness; (4) JetStream critical publication; (5) worker failure observed by the controller; (6) old broker KV revision still held after SIGKILL; (7) new KV revision acquired after TTL; (8) critical wire receipt or journal replay; (9) authenticated local withdrawal decision and committed SQLite target denial; (10) worker report and independent read-only denial observation; (11) confirmed broker ACK; and (12) critical replay cursor advancement. Publication, ACK, cursor, pre-existing target hold, and native status are distinct from a newly committed local withdrawal denial. The current diagnostic records some of these only as final-state readbacks, so it cannot claim a target-side enforcement time or an independent T4/T5 acceptance time.

The broker lease is a single-broker identity exclusion mechanism, not the native target ownership home. `IdentityLease.claim()` uses `kv.create()` and retries every second for up to 45 s. SIGKILL skips `release()`; the FILE KV key remains until its 30 s TTL. `agent_runtime.run()` waits for this claim before starting either critical wire or critical replay. The target owner generation in `ownership.py` is separate. Critical replay checks the lease before a local audit decision and then advances `critical_recovery_cursor` in a later SQLite transaction. Critical wire checks the lease before decision and before ACK, but there is no cross-broker generation barrier at the target and broker consumer. Thus a shorter TTL, forced delete, or preclaim processing alone cannot meet the security invariants.

## Non-negotiable invariants

- A retired or stale owner commits zero protected writes, even if its broker connection, local clock, cached approval, or earlier lease check still appears valid.
- A retired generation advances zero authoritative replay cursors. A delayed JetStream ACK cannot advance the effective cursor of the new generation or stand in for witnessed withdrawal receipt.
- Native T4/T5 inclusion and status remain authoritative. Unknown, unavailable, stale, contradictory, or unverified Trust status denies new protected writes.
- A valid withdrawal produces a durable, exact-scope denial for each eligible agent. Broker publication, ACK, cursor movement and a pre-existing target hold do not count as that denial.
- Once a target hold or withdrawal tombstone is durable, replay, owner transfer, snapshot restoration, or an old plan cannot silently reopen it. A positive reopening operation needs a separate reviewed higher-fence native authority path.
- Existing UUID identity exclusion, KV revision checks, local approvals, target fences, and commit-before-ACK behavior cannot be weakened to satisfy the deadline.

## Competing approaches and consistency implications

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Recover an abandoned KV lease early | Remove most of the 30 s wait | A process can be partitioned or paused rather than dead. A broker-side timeout or controller PID observation cannot prove the old owner is fenced at every protected writer, replay cursor and ACK lane. Forced deletion permits concurrent sessions. | Reject without an independently committed fencing barrier. |
| Generation-fenced claim transfer | Permit fast successor claim | Requires a monotonic, durable generation at a failure-independent home; every protected target must compare it inside its write transaction. Critical replay cursor and broker ACK/consumer state must also reject retired generations, including delayed ACKs and restored snapshots. Transfer must establish the barrier before new authority and must keep unknown Trust restrictive. | Candidate contract for Main Astra review; no encoding or authority approved. |
| Independent withdrawal safety channel | Deliver native T4/T5-witnessed withdrawal to every target without waiting for Mesh lease | Receiver credentials, witness receipt quorum, exact event scope, local durable tombstone, and deny precedence must be defined. Concurrent old and new receivers may both submit the same denial; idempotence is safe only if positive writes cannot race past it. Mesh ACK and cursor stay behind normal ownership. | Promising for denial latency, but requires a separately reviewed target transaction and independent custody. |
| Preemptive deny-only target holds | Quarantine quickly on loss of owner or Trust | A generic hold is not evidence that the specific withdrawal was received. It may prevent writes but cannot satisfy withdrawal-to-durable-denial reporting. Any reopen path requires a new reviewed authority cut and higher fence; an old generation cannot clear it. | Retain as safety backstop, not acceptance substitution. |
| Crash-consistent processing and replay | Avoid lost withdrawal and replay lag after claim | Denial and cursor are currently separate commits; a crash between them must replay idempotently. ## Contract proposed for Main Astra review before implementation

1. Name the generation authority and its persistence/rollback model. Transfer uses a compare-and-swap at that authority. A new generation is effective only after every enrolled protected target has durably installed a fence at least as high as it; unavailable targets remain held and ineligible.
2. Bind each protected write to the generation, exact plan, native T4/T5 cut, local approval, target fence and deadline inside the final target transaction. A stale or unknown generation, missing Trust status, expired cut or conflicting withdrawal denies. No old owner can commit after the transfer barrier.
3. Define a deny-only withdrawal transaction that verifies native T4/T5 witnessed receipt and exact scope, inserts an immutable tombstone, raises a target hold/fence when applicable, and commits the audit atomically. Repetition is idempotent; no ordinary or successor positive path may silently reopen a hold.
4. Define authoritative replay cursor ownership separately from broker ACK. A cursor may advance only after the exact denial is durably committed and after a current-generation check in the same authority transaction or equivalent fenced commit. A delayed old ACK must not advance the authoritative floor; broker consumer metadata is transport state, never withdrawal receipt or target authority.
5. Specify ordering and fault tests for live/paused/partitioned old owner, delayed ACK, duplicate delivery, crash between denial and cursor, rollback/snapshot restore, mixed publisher sequence, broker restart and T4/T5 unknown/contradictory status. Require independent read-only state before observer traffic.

## Compatibility and migration implications

This proposal does not assign wire fields or migration code. A versioned design must name the exact owner generation identifier and authority, then define how it maps to the existing KV key revision, `ownership` generation, target fence, local audit, ordinary/critical recovery cursors, and JetStream durable consumer. These values currently have different scopes and cannot be equated by casting or by reusing a UUID. Existing signed Finding and scoped withdrawal payload bytes, event IDs, publisher sequence rules, native T4/T5 witness rules, and local exact-plan approvals should remain unchanged. If a new signed envelope or receipt is necessary, it needs a new version and strict downgrade rejection.

An older worker or target that cannot verify the new generation must fail closed and remain ineligible; mixed-version rolling deployment cannot allow a successor to claim fast while an older writer is still enrolled. Migration must persist the last accepted generation and denial tombstones before enabling transfer, and must identify every target, coordinator, consumer, and restore path in the cohort. The critical replay cursor and local denial currently use separate SQLite commits. A revised schema must either commit them atomically under a current-generation compare or prove an equivalent fenced transaction; an existing cursor row cannot be assumed safe merely because it is increasing. A failed ACK must remain replayable, and a broker consumer floor remains observational transport state.

## Rollback and recovery risks

Rolling back code while leaving a higher durable generation could let an old binary ignore a fence. Rollback therefore must keep targets held and reject the older binary until compatibility is proved. Restoring a database snapshot can lower the generation or remove a tombstone; an independent monotonic anchor and restore protocol are required before positive authority resumes. A partially installed transfer barrier cannot be called complete: every unresponsive target remains held, with no new write authority. A network partition can make both old and new workers appear healthy locally; the failure detector is an alert source, not proof of fencing. An ACK arriving from a retired session after transfer may update broker metadata even though the local denial or target state is absent; the authoritative cursor must reject it. None of these risks is solved by reducing the KV TTL.

## Required tests before contract implementation is accepted

1. Retain the original Phase 4E and 4F failing tests and raw traces. Run at least three entirely fresh pre-intake SIGKILL plus broker-restart trials with ordinary backlog and both required withdrawal types. Report every eligible agent and both withdrawal pairs, from submission and restored connectivity to independent read-only SQLite denial. Require every eligible pair at or below 5,000 ms; report every failure. Include an agent-alive broker restart control.
2. Race a paused or partitioned old session against a successor. After transfer, verify zero old-generation protected writes and zero authoritative old-generation cursor advances in independent target, audit and cursor readback. Delay an old ACK across transfer and drop the successor ACK. Check broker floor separately without counting it as receipt.
3. Crash immediately before denial commit, after denial but before cursor, after cursor, and before/after ACK. Duplicate and reorder delivery, replay the mixed publisher sequence, and restore older local and broker snapshots. Require exact one logical denial, monotonic authoritative cursor after durable decision, and no loss of a valid withdrawal.
4. Cut T4 or T5 availability or provide stale/contradictory views during transfer and final target commit. New protected writes deny; previously durable holds remain. Verify every protected target and all nine routes before any observer-triggered mutation. An unavailable target is reported unobserved and held, never silently counted safe.
5. Exercise owner transfer with ordinary saturation, a suppressed withdrawal, broker restart, network partition/rejoin, and at least 3, 10 and 100 simulated agents where resources permit. Record per-stage monotonic times, all-agent maximum and p50/p95/p99, CPU/RSS, exact source and image pins, and censored isolation intervals. Physical multi-host and independently administered witness custody require separate validation.

No contract-changing implementation is justified until Main Astra accepts a concrete authority, fencing and ACK/cursor design. The existing approximately 30 s fail-closed wait remains preferable to an unfenced fast takeover. The five-second gate remains failed; this is a release blocker, not an authorization to weaken the lease.
