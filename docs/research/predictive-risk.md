# Predictive risk and exposure

AEGIS Research R1 · 19 November 2026 · Research and experiment design

## Finding

Aegis can plausibly reduce exposure before a vulnerability is disclosed. Removing unnecessary entry points, constraining workload identities, and enforcing network boundaries can reduce what an attacker could reach if a workload were compromised. This is a testable, conditional claim about a deployment. The reviewed evidence does not establish that Aegis can predict which undisclosed flaw will exist, whether someone will discover it, or the probability of exploitation against a particular deployment.

The most credible starting point is a deployment-specific reachability graph that explains which approved hardening action would remove access to a critical asset. Compare it with simple configuration rules under the same change budget. The graph earns its complexity only if independent checks show fewer reachable paths or critical assets, with acceptable service impact. Use EPSS, KEV, and SSVC for a separate known-vulnerability queue. The experiments below are proposed. No performance or predictive results have been measured.

At the repository baseline, Aegis Mesh distributes signed findings and Aegis Rapid independently verifies them, matches simulated local inventory, and records dry-run decisions. The signed finding schema assumes a vulnerability ID and exact affected versions. A future posture assessment would need its own versioned evidence record tied to a deployment snapshot. Local policy would retain authority. An evidence digest supports provenance, while independent tests establish whether a claimed control works. [Aegis architecture](../../architecture.md), [Aegis threat model](../../threat-model.md)

## What the evidence supports

### Surface, paths, and code reachability

Attack surface describes resources through which a system can be interacted with, including entry points, channels, and exposed data. Entry counts can be a useful inventory check. A public endpoint that can act with a broad service identity may matter more than several restricted endpoints. Model paths as sequences of conditions across network, identity, application, and resource boundaries. A route and a permission may both be required, while separate authorization routes may provide alternatives. Logic-based systems such as MulVAL demonstrate how to derive chains from explicit facts and interaction rules. Their conclusions depend on the completeness of those facts and rules. [Manadhata et al., *A Formal Model for a System’s Attack Surface*](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~wing/publications/ManadhataKaynarWing07.pdf), [Ou et al., *MulVAL*](https://static.usenix.org/publications/library/proceedings/sec05/tech/full_papers/ou/ou_html/index.html)

For a disclosed flaw, distinguish **affected build present**, **affected code callable from a specified entry point**, and **flaw-specific preconditions satisfied**. A package match establishes the first claim when identity and version are correct. A static call graph can support the second, subject to its build inputs and modeling of dynamic dispatch, plugins, native code, or runtime configuration. An executed trace proves that a call occurred in that run. An unobserved call may belong to a rare or untested workflow. A path to a function gives limited evidence about input control, authentication, feature flags, or compensating controls. Use build digest, platform, entry point, configuration, observation window, and evidence date when recording these conclusions. Treat undocumented or stale coverage as unknown. VEX can communicate a scoped applicability judgment with a justification. [Ponta et al., *Beyond Metadata*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.05893), [OSV-Scanner call analysis](https://google.github.io/osv-scanner/usage/scan-source), [CISA VEX minimum requirements](https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/2023-04/minimum-requirements-for-vex-508c.pdf)

### Runtime permissions and least privilege

Record configured permissions, effective permissions, observed use, and business-required permissions separately. Effective permissions include conditional and inherited grants, resource policies, usable workload credentials, and enforced deny rules. Observations help identify candidates for review. Test required operations across startup, upgrades, rotation, failover, recovery, and scheduled jobs before removing access, and budget for rollback and exceptions. For workload containment, consider narrower identities, dedicated credentials, fewer mounted secrets and host capabilities, a restricted container profile, and explicit ingress and egress. Verify the actual policy and network plugin behavior with positive and negative checks. Kubernetes NetworkPolicy depends on plugin support and has protocol and traffic limitations. [NIST SP 800-207](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/207/final), [Kubernetes NetworkPolicy](https://kubernetes.io/docs/concepts/services-networking/network-policies/), [Kubernetes Pod Security Standards](https://kubernetes.io/docs/concepts/security/pod-security-standards/), [AWS IAM Access Analyzer policy generation](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/IAM/latest/UserGuide/access-analyzer-policy-generation.html)

### Known-vulnerability signals and local context

| Signal | What it measures | How Aegis would use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EPSS | A daily probability of observed in-the-wild exploitation activity for a published CVE over the next 30 days. The percentile is the score’s relative position. | Use the dated probability to order comparable known findings. Preserve the model version and distinguish missing scores from zero. Local reachability and impact need their own evidence. [FIRST EPSS FAQ](https://www.first.org/epss/faq), [score archive](https://www.first.org/epss/data) |
| CISA KEV | An evidence-backed catalog of CVEs known to have been exploited. Catalog addition is a reporting event. | Escalate applicable entries through local policy. Keep unresolved applicability visible and retain any relevant remediation deadline. Absence from the catalog leaves exploitation status open. [CISA KEV data and history](https://github.com/cisagov/kev-data), [FIRST guidance on KEV](https://www.first.org/epss/using-epss) |
| SSVC | A stakeholder-specific decision tree. CISA’s variant uses exploitation status, automatability, technical impact, mission prevalence, and public well-being impact to assign a response category. CERT/CC’s deployer model has its own decision points. | Choose and pin a tree version. Record the branch inputs, unknowns, and rationale. Use the category to guide action and review. [CISA SSVC guide](https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/publications/cisa-ssvc-guide%20508c.pdf), [CERT/CC deployer model](https://certcc.github.io/SSVC/howto/deployer_tree/) |
| CVSS and local context | CVSS expresses technical severity under base, threat, and environmental metrics. Local context includes deployment exposure, build applicability, impact on critical assets, and verified controls. | Record CVSS version and vector as severity evidence. Use the local evidence to explain which affected instances to address first. [FIRST CVSS v4.0 specification](https://www.first.org/cvss/v4.0/specification-document) |

For example, two instances on the same affected build share an EPSS value. An instance with a verified public request route and broad data access can warrant earlier action than one with restricted ingress and a scoped identity. A missing call trace should prompt an evidence check when the instance matters. Keep confirmed exploitation, severity, conditional access, and business impact visible as distinct inputs. Multiplying their scores would imply a local compromise probability without a supporting causal model or calibration data.

### Limits before disclosure

Before disclosure, configuration and runtime evidence can identify opportunities for reducing access. They cannot enumerate the undisclosed flaws that might matter or establish how often each weakness will be exploited. Conditional assessments can start from a replaceable assumption such as loss of a public-facing workload identity, then ask what resources would be accessible. Any direct impact on the entry workload remains possible, and the conclusion depends on the chosen starting state. Bugs that invalidate the modeled control itself, such as an isolation failure that invalidates a trust boundary, require separate scenarios and can invalidate the ranking.

Public incident records cover cases that were detected and disclosed. Detection capability, reporting practices, product adoption, and researcher attention can change which cases are visible. Later disclosures provide retrospective examples and timing evidence. They leave true negatives and the population of unknown flaws unobserved. A vulnerability hidden from an evaluator in a lab is a useful blind test of conditional containment, with external validity limited to the sampled assumptions. [Google Project Zero analysis of detection and disclosure bias](https://projectzero.google/2022/04/the-more-you-know-more-you-know-you.html)

## Three explainable architectures

Compare the architectures on the same snapshot, asset criticality, admissible change catalog, and availability requirements. A candidate is a small, locally approved change with a recorded scope and rollback, such as disabling an unused listener, narrowing a service grant, or adding a scoped egress rule. Supply the reachability view with effective policy and declared topology first. Add observed use and build-level call evidence as independently testable inputs. Record edge type, source, observation time, applicability, and state as confirmed, blocked, or unknown. A confirmed graph path means the stated conditions hold within the model.

| Architecture | Decision and explanation | Cost and failure modes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Ordered configuration rules** | For each change, flag the highest applicable condition in this order. Public reach plus an effective grant to a critical asset, unused public entry point, unused broad grant to another asset, then other reviewable hardening. “Unused” requires owner evidence and an adequate observation window. Keep uncertain matches visible for review. Break ties by criticality and stable candidate ID. Return the matched rule, inputs, and a recommended change. | Low refresh and review cost. Simple rules catch obvious exposures and may perform well on typical environments. They miss transitive access and may repeatedly prioritize work on the same destination. |
| **B. Typed reachability graph** | Use Boolean prerequisites to derive entry-to-asset reachability over network, identity, and policy facts. Track confirmed pairs and possible pairs. A possible route can include unresolved facts if its prerequisites could hold together. Rank by the total impact weight of pairs that were confirmed before the change and have no possible route afterward. Break ties by the weight of removed possible pairs, then stable candidate ID. Send changes with only uncertain benefit to review. Return a path witness, the invalidated conditions, alternate routes, and missing facts. | Traversal and incremental refresh add cost. Exact path enumeration can be exponential, and partial policy semantics can give wrong answers. Optimize endpoint reachability in the common query. Enumerate paths with explicit bounds for small fixtures and sampled explanations. |
| **C. Scenario-weighted graph with sensitivity analysis** | Reuse B's graph under declared starting conditions, such as assumed loss of an internet-facing, partner-facing, or internal workload identity. For each scenario, use the impact-weighted reduction in reachable entry-to-asset pairs as benefit. Rank by the minimum weighted benefit across the agreed weight range and consistent completions of unknown facts. Preserve the same facts before and after each hypothetical change unless the action changes them. Break ties by benefit at the nominal weights using the same worst-case treatment of unknowns, then stable candidate ID. Return per-scenario effects, evidence, and assumptions that would change the ordering. | More graph evaluations and more judgment to maintain. Wide ranges can produce many ties, while narrow ranges can hide blind spots. Scenario weights express a planning preference. Use held-out case frequencies only to evaluate sensitivity and generalization. |

For all three, reject changes that violate the declared functional constraints and retain a separate evidence-review queue for abstentions. In B and C, asset weights are proposed as 1, 3, and 9 for low, important, and critical assets. Count a reachable asset once per starting state. If a bounded search cannot resolve a case or C cannot compute a defensible bound, disclose it as indeterminate. A and B are the first comparison. C is justified only if mixed starting conditions materially change actions and it improves held-out outcomes enough to pay for its additional queries and review.

For example, a public-facing demo service may have two independently authorized routes to a protected test store. Removing its direct grant blocks one route. B can show that the store remains reachable through the second route and identify the unresolved conditions. C can additionally show whether this change matters under a different starting identity. The finding is conditional on the permissions and boundaries in the snapshot.

Patching and removing obsolete components, service isolation, default-deny networking, and scoped identities provide credible alternatives to predictive ranking. If fixed policies deliver equivalent containment within service and change budgets, a graph may still help explain exceptions, but it would lack a case as a separate prevention system. ## Reproducible experiment design

### Common setup

Use a synthetic state model, plus a disposable local Kubernetes fixture with ordinary service calls, stub functions, and dummy resources. A simulated loss of a workload is an input state in the model. In the fixture, the observer uses intended test identities and harmless requests to verify effective allow and deny decisions. Use no exploit execution, public targets, or production access. Keep the oracle independent of each ranker. An evaluator-authored manifest defines allowed business operations, expected reachable states, and the full bounded candidate universe. Check these statements with benign runtime probes and exact enumeration of fixture states. Validate discrepancies before scoring and report unresolved cases.

Generate 120 small fixtures from six equally represented families. Use seeds 101–120 for each family. The families cover public versus private entry, alternate routes, conditional or inherited permissions, dormant features and dynamic calls, required rare jobs, and drift or stale inventory. Each fixture uses three entry states (internet, partner, and internal), up to eight workloads, three dummy protected endpoints, and at least five available changes. Assign the three asset weights from low through critical. Balance positive and negative cases within families and report the observed label prevalence separately when replaying public data. Use seeds 101–110 for development (60 fixtures) and 111–120 for test (60 fixtures). As a structural generalization check, hold out each of the alternate-route and rare-job families in separate folds, using only the other families’ development fixtures for tuning and the omitted family’s test fixtures for evaluation.

Before seeing test labels, freeze the fixture generator, candidate IDs, rules and thresholds, graph schema, impact weights, workload traces, and observation and censoring rules in a run manifest. For C use nominal starting-state weights of 0.60, 0.25, and 0.15 for internet, partner, and internal starts. Evaluate the feasible simplex with each weight within 0.15 of its nominal value, using its vertices for linear benefit. Keep these weights fixed in test. For lab replay use all ten test seeds per family and publish every run’s graph and oracle outputs separately. Record the harness revision, build flags, image and advisory file digests, policy and CNI versions, machine and kernel details, UTC timestamps, resource limits, and random seeds. Publish per-case predictions, score explanations, denominators, changes selected, and raw measurements with the report. The current document specifies the protocol. Fixture generation, instrumentation, and any commercial product replay are future work.

### E1. Reachability precision and coverage

Use inert functions in a small Go application as affected-symbol surrogates. Include cases where a dependency is absent, present with no callable target, callable only from a private entry, reachable through a rare branch, and reachable from a public entry. Cover more than one build and configuration. Have a fixture-owned entry-point map and branch-specific benign tests provide independent labels for the tuple of build, entry point, and affected symbol. Separately score the entry-to-protected-asset tuples in the state model and fixture.

Compare package-only matching, supported static call analysis, runtime observation alone, and static plus runtime evidence. Run the graph with and without runtime and call evidence. A only predicts direct configuration conditions. Report its coverage for any path or symbol label it cannot estimate. Evaluate exact component identity separately so a version-matching error is distinguishable from a reachability error. Deliberately withhold the rare workflow from the observation stream while keeping it in the oracle to test unsafe suppression.

For both layers, report true and false positives and negatives and abstentions. Use FPR = FP / (FP + TN), FNR = FN / (FN + TP), and precision = TP / (TP + FP) on resolved predictions. Report coverage against all oracle-labeled cases and give bounds that respectively treat abstentions per class as correct or incorrect. For the operational view, score review-worthy unknowns as alerts, then also show the risk of closing them as negative. Publish counts by family, support tier, and freshness status. Exclude unresolved oracle labels from point estimates and publish their count and sensitivity bounds.

### E2. Known-issue ranking with dated inputs

Use one forecast per eligible published CVE. At midnight UTC on the first Monday after public availability, take only records demonstrably available by that cutoff and use the latest EPSS file published before it. Use archived CVE List commits or advisories and the CISA KEV commit then visible. Retain each dated EPSS file and its version header. Exclude CVEs already in KEV at the cutoff from the future-addition ranking. Evaluate separately whether the local policy retains already-listed applicable CVEs for review. Deduplicate aliases by canonical CVE. Pre-register the development cutoffs from 7 April to 24 November 2025 and test cutoffs from 5 January to 5 October 2026, inclusive. Allow a full 30-day follow-up and report the test cohorts before and after the June 2026 EPSS version change. Exclude rows with unverified snapshot provenance or incomplete follow-up and report the counts. [CVE List archive](https://github.com/CVEProject/cvelistV5), [CISA history](https://github.com/cisagov/kev-data), [FIRST historical EPSS files](https://www.first.org/epss/data)

Rank by CVSS base, EPSS, and the pinned SSVC decision order where point-in-time inputs exist. Use stable tie-breaking and report missing-data coverage and same-cohort comparisons. The public label is first KEV addition during the subsequent 30 days. Measure average precision, precision at the top 5% and 10%, and recall at those same budgets. Any FPR or FNR for this label describes errors predicting catalog addition. Case ascertainment and additions can lag exploitation, and public data cannot reproduce EPSS calibration against its underlying telemetry.

For a local comparison, pair fixture instances that share the same synthetic advisory and threat tier but differ in entry-point reachability, privilege, asset impact, or effective controls. Rank affected instances using advisory severity and threat inputs alone, then with local evidence added. Use benign oracle checks and the fixture’s business-impact labels to assign relevance by the weight of accessible protected assets when the specified entry and affected-symbol conditions hold. Report nDCG@5 and missed critical instances at a five-item review budget. Repeat on operator records only if an independently adjudicated dated inventory exists. Use records collected after the fact only for sensitivity analysis and disclose their timing.

### E3. Exposure reduction and action ranking before disclosure

Hide CVE and affected-symbol information from all architecture inputs. In the isolated fixture, encode each held-out compromise assumption as a starting state with scoped, predeclared permissions to dummy assets. Include alternate routes, credential rotation, rare required jobs, stale edges, and two stress cases where a modeled control is assumed lost. For each fixture, independently score admissible candidates by the post-change reduction in impact-weighted reachable entry-to-asset pairs, using the oracle. Candidates that break required functions have zero relevance and count as regressions. Measure action ranking with nDCG@5 using that graded reduction, the best candidate selected at rank one, and recall of beneficial candidates within each budget.

Compare A, B, and C with no change, equal-budget random selection, a fixed native hardening order, and a usage-only permission review order. The native order removes an unused public listener, restricts network access, scopes workload identity, restricts pod privilege, and then removes other unused access, skipping unavailable changes. The usage baseline sorts permission candidates by number of grants still unobserved, with owner-required grants excluded. Break ties by stable ID. Average random selection over 100 seeded draws per fixture. Apply each arm from the same clean fixture at budgets of one, three, and five changes. Recompute architecture rankings after each selected change. Freeze a no-graph change order before test and allow earlier termination when there is no eligible action. Report actual changes, owner review time and any unspent budget.

Count distinct bounded paths before and after changes over the same stable edge IDs, start states, and protected destinations. Count a path as a simple sequence of up to four typed state transitions with its prerequisites satisfied. Collapse duplicate representations and publish the truncation rule. Report the fraction remaining, new paths, distinct reachable critical entry-to-asset pairs, and loss of required business flows. For zero baseline paths, report absolute counts and mark the fraction unavailable. Run longer-route sensitivity at six transitions on small fixtures. Use distinct reachable critical pairs as the primary outcome because path counts can grow through alternate routes even when the number of exposed assets is unchanged. Disclose each new critical path and record its cause. Verify functionality after every applied change and again after rollback. Measure results for ordinary cases and each stress scenario separately.

### E4. Runtime and computation overhead

Use the same fixture image, dataset, CPU allocations, and request schedule per configuration. Include no observer, rules or graph collection alone, observer in monitoring mode, native controls alone, and native controls plus the observer. Keep change templates and ordinary service load fixed across measurements. Run ten randomized paired repetitions after a five-minute warm-up, with fifteen minutes of measurement each at idle, 50%, and 80% of the baseline’s sustainable throughput without an observer. Determine sustainable throughput for each configuration during a separate rate sweep using the highest rate that meets the fixture’s frozen latency and error-rate SLOs. Include a burst and workflow rotation in every loaded run. Set the fixture SLOs before the pilot from the declared workload needs and publish them.

Report request p50/p95/p99, throughput at the fixture SLO, error rate, collector event rate and drop rate, application and observer CPU, peak resident memory, and telemetry bytes per host per hour. Separately benchmark graph build, single-change recomputation, a 1% edge-churn batch, and a five-candidate ranking at 1,000, 10,000, and 100,000 facts. Track wall time, CPU-seconds, peak memory, stored bytes, and time to converge after a policy update. Use five cold and ten warm runs per size and publish query failures and timeouts. Attribute runtime monitor costs separately from batch analysis and from enforcement by native controls. Use noisy or dropped telemetry in the subsequent accuracy sensitivity checks.

### Decision criteria

Register the comparison thresholds before held-out runs. Give all interventions equal budgets and use a validation-selected strongest simple baseline from A, native hardening, and usage-guided review. On E3 at budget three, advance the graph only if it leaves at least 20% fewer independently verified reachable critical pairs than that baseline, measured as the relative reduction in mean residual count across the same fixtures. Require a paired 95% confidence interval for the absolute reduction to exclude zero. If the baseline leaves zero on average, it has already met the primary containment objective. Require zero observed critical-flow regressions and no new verified critical paths in the held-out default scenarios. Publish all stress-scenario failures.

For E1, set operational limits of at most 10% FNR and 25% FPR at at least 90% coverage for asset reachability on supported fixtures. Treat misses with uncertain inventory as a separate analysis and keep their contribution visible. For E4, use proposed budgets of at most 5% relative p95 request-latency increase, 3% relative throughput loss, and 1% event loss at the higher planned load, plus p95 warm ranking within 60 seconds at 100,000 facts. Report instance resource limits and observed memory and CPU consumption alongside these gates. The numbers are pilot decision thresholds and should be frozen before testing. Use 2,000 paired bootstrap resamples clustered by fixture, load-run pair, or forecast week as appropriate. Preserve case pairing, report intervals and sample sizes, and treat a wide or unstable interval as inconclusive. Advance C only if it improves on B for the same practical objective in the held-out mixed-start tests and meets the same overhead and service budgets.

## What would falsify the case for Aegis

- **Basic controls already capture the benefit.** If fixed hardening removes the same independently verified critical reachability at comparable cost, the added ranking and data pipeline have little preventive value.
- **The model verifies its own assumptions.** Removing an edge from a graph can lower its score while effective permissions, alternate paths, or changed identities preserve access. Blinded oracle checks and post-change probes decide the outcome.
- **Incomplete or drifting inventory dominates.** Identity resolution errors, stale grants, dynamic dispatch, and missing network state can cause recommendations to oscillate or hide reachable resources. If conservative abstention leaves little coverage, a smaller supported scope may be all that can be defended.
- **Normal operations need seemingly unused access.** Rare jobs and break-glass procedures can reverse the security benefit through availability failure or blanket exceptions. Measure required-flow failures, review time, and rollback outcomes.
- **The threat scenario drives the result.** A control may block movement from a public workload and have little effect on an insider, a compromised build, or a failure of the isolation layer. If rankings reverse under plausible alternative starts, a single aggregate ordering gives false precision.
- **The benchmark measures disclosure or fixture construction.** KEV dates reflect curation, and synthetic scenarios reflect choices by the evaluator. Better rankings on either can coexist with no improvement in local future outcomes. Field evidence would require a prospective, time-stamped deployment study with independent outcome review.

The first decision is whether Aegis can justify an exposure review that an operator can verify and act on before disclosure. The evidence needed is a reproducible reduction in reachable critical assets and preserved required functions at a fixed change budget. A separate claim about forecasting specific undisclosed vulnerabilities would require a defensible outcome and sampling process absent from the current evidence.
