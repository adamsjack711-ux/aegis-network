# Phase 4D Journal Integrity

**T4 disposition.** The witnessed Mesh profile closes the coordinator-only completeness dependency identified in 4C-01 under the trust assumptions below. The profile is verified on a disposable, single-host lab. Deployment acceptance requires independently operated authorities and an enforced cutover to the witnessed writer profile. The existing Phase 4A journal continues to recover transport delivery for the legacy lab. [Phase 4C finding](phase4c-adversarial.md#4c-01--high--journal-completeness-has-no-independent-trust-source) · [Phase 4A handoff](phase4a-distributed.md)

## Trust model

The Coordinator orders signed Mesh findings and scoped withdrawals and signs tree proposals. Publishers and revokers send the same signed envelope directly to independent operators. Each operator owns its admission and coverage key, its intake journal and its rollback-protected pin. The agent pins the coordinator key, operator keys, log ID, cohort, epoch and freshness parameters, and exact historical publisher grants as one policy digest. Archive hosts can be untrusted for integrity. Authenticated cohort access protects the event data. An independent monitor retains coordinator signatures and checks merge promises along a separate path.

The tested policy uses four operators, a quorum of three and a bound of one Byzantine operator. Two quorums intersect in at least two operators. At least one of those two is honest. The policy constructor enforces `2q − n > f` and `q ≤ n − f`, with distinct operator and coordinator keys. This calculation assumes distinct administrative control and durable, non-rollbackable operator state. Multiple keys held by one administrator constitute one failure domain. The integrity argument assumes Ed25519 signature unforgeability, SHA-256 collision and second-preimage resistance, sound clock bounds, and continuity of protected state.

Coverage is defined over quorum-receipted envelopes in the pinned stream through a sealed cutoff. A malformed or never-receipted submission may remain outside that coverage. Authorized issuers can sign false claims. Independent receipt or archive unavailability can cause a hold. A disconnected agent learns outside events on reconnect. Its prior authority lasts at most to its existing bounded deadline, and observed refresh failure clears eligibility. ## Protocol

1. **Admit directly.** Each operator validates the envelope signature, role, publisher scope and validity at its trusted time bounds. It durably records the canonical envelope and an admission over the policy digest, UTC epoch and envelope digest before releasing its Ed25519 vote. A receipt consists of a canonical set of at least three operator votes for the same admission. A same-epoch retry returns the same vote. Previously admitted bytes and votes remain retrievable after publisher or envelope expiry. The epoch cutoff is `(epoch + 1) × epoch_seconds` and the receipt deadline is cutoff plus `merge_seconds`. Once the cutoff has passed an honest operator cannot admit into the earlier epoch.
2. **Append.** The archive allocates dense, zero-based indexes separate from Postgres row positions and publisher sequences. A leaf commits the full envelope and receipt. The reference canonical encoding is UTF-8 JSON with sorted keys and compact separators. Domain-separated SHA-256 hashes commit leaves and internal nodes using the RFC 9162 tree shape. Exact envelope retries return their existing index. Conflicting event IDs or publisher sequences fail closed. [RFC 9162 §2.1](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9162.html#section-2.1)
3. **Witness the sealed cut.** The coordinator signs the policy digest, epoch, tree size and root. After the cutoff, each operator checks the coordinator signature, cutoff age, consistency from its own durable prior state, every new dense index and inclusion proof, and each enrolled admission. It refuses coverage if any of its due intake promises is absent. It durably pins the new state before releasing a signature over the exact coordinator proposal. If a due receipted event is absent, at least one honest operator in every proposed coverage quorum holds its admission promise and refuses to sign. If two inconsistent proposals at the same or different sizes both obtain a quorum, their common honest operator would have had to violate its persisted consistency rule.
4. **Reconcile.** The agent polls independent witness endpoints, checks their signatures and freshness, and pins the observed cut before requesting proof or replay. An unresolved cut remains pinned across restart. The agent requires a valid consistency proof from its previously verified anchor, complete pages at the selected head, and valid admission and inclusion for every missing leaf. It persists the reconciled head and leaf prefix together in SQLite. The prefix supplies exact findings and durable tombstones keyed by target publisher, artifact ID and finding-body digest. A corrupt local cache can be reconstructed from a verified mirror using the protected pin. An older or incompatible backup cannot authorize a rollback.
5. **Bind the writer.** `MeshIntake(..., journal=journal_client)` makes the witnessed cut mandatory for the Phase 3C fixture writer. Every refresh checks the broker lease, the reconciled exact signed finding, and local Rapid and Evidence policy. The approved plan binds the checkpoint digest and expires by the earliest journal or existing plan limit. A changed cut requires a new exact approval, including an empty epoch with the same root. The target checks current status before the guarded write and before commit. In-process checkpoint changes and guarded writes share a lock. The proxy checks the monotonic deadline during request and response admission. A later withdrawal learned after the canary blocks peer writes. The existing observation path establishes restrictive fixture recovery.
6. **Monitor.** An independent observer accepts signed heads from multiple paths, retains signed conflicts at the same size or in the same epoch, and checks consistency across sizes. It retains verified receipt certificates and records missing inclusion at the deadline using its own trusted time. A pair of conflicting signed heads is attributable evidence. Missing or invalid proof shows the view could not be verified. The monitor records late proof separately so it cannot establish timely delivery after the fact.

The freshness limit is based on cutoff age, using the conservative UTC bound and a local monotonic deadline. Signing again over the same cutoff cannot extend it. Once a withdrawal is receipted in epoch `e`, a usable cut at or beyond the cutoff of `e` includes the withdrawal. An older cut can remain eligible only through its fixed cutoff plus the configured maximum age. A withdrawal unknown to a writer can therefore race a locally valid commit inside that window.

## Integration requirements

- **Enrollment and cutover.** Provision one signed policy and stream per enrolled Mesh cohort, with distinct public keys and independently controlled service credentials. Create the first witnessed prefix from a reviewed migration and account for every known denial. A legacy Postgres cursor supplies transport recovery only. An enrollment enabling protected writes must require a witnessed journal and omit fallback to the legacy-only writer. The reference opt-in is the `journal` argument to `MeshIntake`. Phase 4A and the existing legacy scripts retain their lab behavior.
- **Actors and transports.** Mount `archive_router(archive, authorize)` and `witness_router(witness, local_archive, authorize)` in separately deployed FastAPI services. The supplied authorization dependency must authenticate the caller and require operation-specific and cohort-scoped permissions. Exposing the archive and intake endpoints requires mTLS or equivalent authenticated transport, per-role credentials, request and concurrency budgets, and independently controlled endpoints. Use signed/cohort-authorized forwarding only, keep witness key material out of coordinator processes, and keep immutable archives away from a shared restore or administrative domain. Reference routes cap request and response bytes. Configure real clients with bounded timeouts and reserve capacity for withdrawals and proofs.
- **Publication.** Send signed events directly to operators with `HTTPWitness.admit`, collect matching votes with `collect_receipt`, and append certified leaves in identical order to the log and authorized mirrors using `Archive.append` or `HTTPArchive.append`. A witness can supply its retained envelope and vote using `HTTPWitness.admitted` during coordinator recovery. After seal, construct the signed proposal with `Archive.propose` and request coverage through `HTTPWitness.cosign`. The relying client uses `JournalClient(policy, HTTPHeads(policy, independent_witnesses), archives, protected_pin, cache_path, clock)` where `archives` is a tuple of independent `HTTPArchive` sources. A scheduler must publish sealed cuts, including empty epochs, and an independent monitor must poll heads and receipts and route alerts.
- **State and recovery.** Provision a unique protected pin per operator and deployment. The reference `SQLitePin` is a compare-and-swap adapter and belongs in a separate, access-controlled restore domain. Production requires a genuinely rollback-resistant external implementation of the `Pin` interface. Operate one active writer for each witness ledger or archive path. A witness with a stale or corrupt ledger stops signing. Restore its exact anchored journal or use an authenticated handover before it rejoins. A structurally unreadable agent database can be recovered by starting with a new cache path and reusing the protected pin. Keep previously accepted Rapid denials and fences through cutover and recovery. The reference replays fixed historical grants, including planned expiration. Authenticated roster or policy rotation, compromise adjudication and historical grant migration require a separate signed handover contract.
- **Freshness and rollout.** Deploy a trusted UTC source with declared uncertainty and a local monotonic clock. Choose an epoch, merge deadline and maximum coverage age with enough margin for witness availability, skew and replay. Catch up offline agents before the one-second Phase 3C transition refresh budget. Treat detected uncertainty, unavailable proofs and missing required streams as holds. Pair independent Evidence status and deployment-wide identity fencing at integration. Those are Phase 4C findings 4C-04 and 4C-02 and are outside the Mesh-journal implementation here.
- **Retention and monitoring.** Preserve signed envelopes, certificates, protected pins and archives through the longest supported offline interval. A partial intake can create a local promise at an honest witness even when the submitter never assembled quorum. Reassemble and merge that promise from independent intake where possible. An unresolved promise can stop fresh coverage. Handling an irrecoverable promise requires authorized disposition. Restrict cohort data and preserve durable monitor alerts and signed head conflicts.

## Measurements and repeatability

The benchmark uses identical signed Mesh events for both paths on one macOS ARM64 host, SQLite and an in-process proof archive. The legacy baseline uses the unmodified Phase 4A journal reconciliation and an HTTPX mock. The replay ends with an exact withdrawal of the first finding. Each trial times direct three-operator admission with archive append, three serial coverage signatures, agent replay and a restart from the cached prefix. The initial cut covers 80 percent of the events. Timings exclude epoch waiting, remote TLS and network latency, and broker delivery. Storage is SQLite file size after the trial. Payload bytes are application JSON before compression and HTTP overhead. The legacy agent file stores policy decisions, while the witnessed cache stores full certified events. A deployed witnessed writer additionally retains its Rapid audit.
