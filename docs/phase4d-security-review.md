## Recovery and fault model

- Existing 4C and mitigation suites exercise expired approved plans, isolation after canary, incomplete observation, stale fences, crashed or unavailable fixtures, and preservation of the restrictive state. They use fresh HTTP and SQLite reads from outside the write transaction.
- The modeled Trust evaluator rejects replayed challenges and stale approvals, clears pending authority on disconnect, holds on older snapshots or a lost anchor, and requires fresh status and local approval after reconnect. It accepts a historical withdrawal backed by admission during the retiring key's valid interval under its simulated trust chain. Actual federation, trusted time, signer and target restore, and live long-duration reconnect through rotation remain unproven.
- A fork detected beyond the promised fault bound is latched in the synthetic local anchor. A quorum can suppress service; one authenticated signature or a current-looking coordinator head cannot fill a missing quorum. An unserved proof is unavailable status, with no inference about motive.
- Combined negative cases use an exact healthy control, a matching durable Rapid decision, a signed cut and separate status stores. The legacy comparison can appraise the same native bundle with an unchanged coordinator view. Every post-canary negative case observes all nine route/replica pairs quarantined before rollback, verifies a terminal `safe_hold` and checks the durable write list contains only the canary. A missing prior Rapid record denies plan preparation with no target transition.
- Synthetic keys, clocks, stores and target fences model the required boundaries. A compromised provisioner, raw target writer, time or restore control, or sufficient colluding administrators remains outside those results. The shadow comparison drops the prototype's pending preparation after each check.
