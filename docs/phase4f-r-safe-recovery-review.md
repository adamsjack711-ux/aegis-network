## Contract disposition

- **Target-side denial:** A locally enrolled, independently T4/T5-verified, exact-scope restrictive target transaction could improve *target protection* while a broker KV lease remains held. It must bind target identity, exact plan/Evidence, current generation/fence, local restrictive capability and proof inside the final transaction. A generic hold or target-owned receipt is a separate target outcome. VAL-R's frozen `revocation_decisions` oracle requires **each agent's own SQLite denial** after its own Trust, signature, inventory and policy evaluation; a target receipt cannot satisfy any of the 72+18 agent pairs. Thus the preferred path in `a51e8a6` does not by itself close the five-second all-agent gate.
- **Generation-fenced positive recovery:** The proposal correctly separates broker KV revision from target-home generation and requires a complete writer barrier plus external continuity anchor. It does not specify an independently administered monotonic anchor, authenticated full writer/cursor roster, exact CAS transaction, mixed-version rollout or rollback recovery sufficiently to grant successor authority. A paused incumbent and late old ACK remain negative tests. No early lease deletion, TTL shortening or preclaim positive action is approved.
- **Crash-safe replay:** Commit exact denial and provenance before authoritative cursor movement; ACK remains transport telemetry. The proposed ledger/cursor contract needs canonical fields, current-generation compare-and-swap, idempotent conflict handling and restored-snapshot anchor. A broker ACK or HTTP receipt cannot stand in for native Trust or local denial.
- **Native T4/T5:** A versioned, independently reviewed one-to-one Mesh revocation→Evidence mapping, issuer scope, witnessed inclusion, current status/freshness, contradiction precedence and local approval binding are prerequisites. Unknown or unavailable authority blocks new positive writes; it cannot become a fabricated withdrawal-specific tombstone. The pre-existing native T4/T5 path remains authoritative.
